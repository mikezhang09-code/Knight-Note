Here's a comprehensive summary of all three interviews with Peter Steinberger, the creator of OpenClaw:

---

## Who is Peter Steinberger?

Peter Steinberger is a veteran software entrepreneur who founded **PSPDFKit**, a global PDF developer tools company he ran for 13 years before selling it roughly four years ago. After the sale, he suffered severe burnout — describing himself as someone who had their "spark sucked out" — and took a three-year break. He came back to coding in April 2025, drawn in by the potential of LLMs and AI agents. He calls himself someone who "came back from retirement to mess with AI."

OpenClaw (which went through the names **Clawdbot → Moltbot → OpenClaw**) started as a hobby hackathon project and became one of the fastest-growing GitHub repositories of all time, accumulating 100,000+ stars and 2 million site visitors in a week, generating more Google searches than Claude Code and Codex combined.

---

## Interview 1: TBPN Live — "First Public Appearance Since Launch" (January 28, 2026)

This was Peter's **first public interview** after OpenClaw went viral, recorded live on TBPN (a tech talk show described by the NYT as "Silicon Valley's newest obsession").

**Key themes:**

- Peter sold his software company after 13 years, then burned out completely. He returned to coding in April 2025 when his "spark came back," and discovered AI tools just as Claude Code launched in beta. He became so hooked he started texting friends at 4 a.m., and they'd reply — all equally obsessed. He founded a meetup called "Claude Code Anonymous" (now renamed "Agents Anonymous").

- OpenClaw started at a hackathon: the original idea was simply to use Claude Code from his phone. He built it for two months and had to stop because it was consuming his life. He built a lot of CLI tools along the way, because "that's where agents are really good" — you have to close the feedback loop so agents can verify their own work.

- OpenClaw's GitHub star growth was described as unprecedented — "a line going straight up" — and Peter joked he needed to call GitHub because he didn't think any project had grown that way before.

- On his philosophy: he prefers the term "agentic engineering" over "vibe coding." His approach is to have fun, try different languages and approaches, and build small useful things. "I always make the joke: I do agentic engineering, and then when it hits 3 a.m., I switch to vibe coding. And then the next day I have regrets."

## Interview 2: The Pragmatic Engineer — "I Ship Code I Don't Read" (January 28, 2026)

This 114-minute interview (published same day as TBPN, but recorded two weeks earlier in London) was hosted by Gergely Orosz and goes deep into Peter's engineering philosophy and workflow.

**Key themes:**

- Peter made over 6,600 commits in January alone. As he described it: "From the commits, it might appear like it's a company. But it's not. This is one dude sitting at home having fun."

- **Letting go of perfectionism**: Running PSPDFKit with 70+ people forced Peter to accept that code wouldn't always match his exact preferences — a mindset that now makes him more effective when working with AI agents, which also don't produce perfectly clean code.

- **Closing the loop**: The secret to working with AI agents is building systems where the agent can verify its own work — tests, feedback, and code all have to be tightly coupled. This was a recurring theme throughout his workflow.

- **"I ship code I don't read"**: Peter describes trusting AI-generated code enough to ship it without line-by-line review — a radical departure from traditional software engineering. He evaluates outputs holistically rather than scrutinizing every line.

- **Engineering taste matters more than ever**: Even when AI writes the code, the human's job is architectural vision, system-level thinking, and dependency selection. If you don't have taste and vision, AI just produces "slop."

- **No plan mode**: Peter explicitly avoids "plan mode" in AI tools, preferring a more conversational, direct approach.

---

## Interview 3: Behind the Craft — "How OpenClaw's Creator Uses AI to Run His Life in 40 Minutes" (February 1, 2026)

This shorter (~38-minute) interview on the "Behind the Craft" podcast focuses on **Peter's personal use cases** for OpenClaw and his hot takes on AI development tools.

**Key themes:**

- Peter's favorite personal OpenClaw use cases include checking in to flights, controlling his home (lights, bed adjustments), monitoring his security camera overnight, and more. The AI acts as a bridge between messaging apps (WhatsApp/iMessage) and his computer.

- Key topics he covered: why he thinks 80% of smartphone apps will disappear, his hot takes on no plan mode, and why MCPs (Model Context Protocols) are not something he uses.

- His memorable quotes: "It's like having a new weird friend that is also really smart and resourceful that lives on your computer." And: "Why should I use MyFitnessPal when I have an infinitely resourceful assistant that already knows I'm making bad decisions at KFC?"

- On avoiding the "agentic trap": "This whole trend of ralphing where agents can create code and run all night just to create the ultimate slop — because what those agents don't yet have is taste. They are spiky smart, but if you don't navigate them well, if you don't have a vision of what you'll build, it's still going to be slop."

- His advice for developers: build a "CLI army" — agents are trained to call and interact with command-line interfaces, so building robust CLIs is how you make agents most effective. System-level thinking, architectural taste, and dependency selection remain the primary value-add for human engineers.

- Peter built 43 projects before OpenClaw went viral — nearly every one being a terminal-first integration with a popular service. He wasn't building random tools; he was building OpenClaw's foundation piece by piece.

---

## Across All Three Interviews: Core Philosophy

- **Privacy/local-first**: OpenClaw runs on your own hardware (e.g., a Mac Mini), keeping your data under your control rather than in corporate clouds.
- **Anti-commercialization**: After OpenClaw went viral, Peter turned down VC investment. He's already financially independent and set up a non-profit foundation so the project can outlive him — his goal is for OpenClaw to become a public good like Linux.
- **Radical transparency on security**: He publicly admits prompt injection is an unsolved problem and invites hackers to attack OpenClaw, believing community scrutiny is the only path to real security.
- **The future of software**: Peter believes natural language will make everyone a developer, and that most apps will be replaced by personalized AI agents built to solve each individual's unique problems.
