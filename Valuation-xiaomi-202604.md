# Xiaomi at 52-week lows: a value investor's deep dive

**Xiaomi Corporation (1810.HK) trades at HKD 30.88 — roughly half its June 2025 peak — creating the most compelling entry point since 2022 for a company generating RMB 39.2 billion in adjusted profit with a rapidly scaling EV business.** The probability-weighted intrinsic value estimate of **HKD 39–40** implies approximately 27% upside, though the margin of safety falls short of the 33% threshold Graham-Buffett purists demand. Xiaomi's transformation from a smartphone discounter into a diversified hardware-internet-automotive conglomerate is real and accelerating, but the stock's discount reflects genuine risks: a collapsing India business, smartphone segment headwinds, memory cost inflation, and a brutally competitive China EV market. The verdict is a **conditional BUY** at a **6/10 conviction level**, suited to a 2–4% portfolio allocation for investors comfortable with China tech risk.

## Five-year financial trajectory reveals a company in metamorphosis

Xiaomi's financial profile has changed dramatically between FY2021 and FY2025. Revenue recovered from the 2022–2023 trough to reach an all-time high of **RMB 457.3 billion** in FY2025, driven by the EV business contributing RMB 103.3B (23% of total) in just its second year of deliveries. Gross margins expanded from 17.0% to 22.3%, reflecting premiumization across all segments. Adjusted net profit nearly doubled from FY2024 to RMB 39.2B, and IFRS net income surged 76% to RMB 41.6B.

The balance sheet is a source of strength. **Net cash stands at approximately RMB 76 billion** (RMB 112.3B in cash and short-term investments minus RMB 36.1B in debt), providing a substantial cushion for continued EV investment. ## The EV business changed the investment thesis entirely

Xiaomi's electric vehicle segment is the single most important variable in any valuation of this company. In just 20 months from first delivery, Xiaomi achieved what most automakers take a decade to accomplish: **411,082 vehicles delivered in 2025, generating RMB 103.3 billion in revenue at a 24.3% gross margin**, and turning an operating profit of RMB 0.9B — transforming from a RMB 6.2B loss in FY2024 to profitability in FY2025.

The SU7 sedan outsold the Tesla Model 3 in China by **29%** (258K vs 200K units), a remarkable feat for a brand with zero automotive heritage. The YU7 SUV, launched in June 2025, contributed 153K deliveries in just six months. Monthly deliveries scaled from ~20,000 in January to over **50,000 in December 2025**, and Lei Jun targets 550,000 deliveries in 2026 with four new models launching, including the company's first extended-range vehicles.

The EV segment's gross margin trajectory is particularly impressive: from 12.5% at launch in Q1 2024 to **25.5% in Q3 2025**, approaching the margins of established automakers like BYD. Average selling price rose to RMB 251,200, with the SU7 Ultra at RMB 529,900 pushing into the premium tier. The capital intensity remains high — Xiaomi raised $5.3B through an equity placement in March 2025 and is constructing a third factory to push annual capacity toward 500,000 units — but the speed of margin improvement suggests the business could become meaningfully FCF-positive by 2027.

The competitive landscape is brutal. **BYD dominates with ~30% of China's NEV market**, while Geely, Tesla, Leapmotor, and Huawei's Aito brand all compete fiercely. Xiaomi ranked #7 with a 3.1% share in 2025, but its growth rate (+195% YoY) was the highest among the top ten. The key risk is that China's EV market is transitioning from subsidy-driven expansion to a mature price war — the 5% NEV purchase tax taking effect in 2026 will test demand resilience.

## Smartphones stall while IoT and internet services deliver steady cash

The smartphone business, still Xiaomi's largest legacy segment at RMB 186.4B, showed concerning weakness in FY2025 with **revenue declining 2.8%** and shipments falling 2% to 165.2 million units. Gross margins compressed to 10.9% (down 1.7pp) due to rising memory costs. In Q4 2025, shipments dropped 11.5% YoY — the sharpest quarterly decline among global top-five vendors.

Xiaomi retained the #3 global position with **13.1% market share** but lost ground to Apple (20%) and Samsung (19.1%), both of which grew 7–8%. Huawei's resurgence to China's #1 smartphone brand (16.4% share, up from #6 in 2023) is compressing Xiaomi's domestic position. The India market — once contributing 45% of global revenue — has collapsed to single digits following ₹4,820 crore (~$545M) in government seizures.

The premiumization strategy shows mixed results. The Xiaomi 15 Ultra sold well globally (58% YoY increase), and **27.6% of China smartphone units** were in the premium tier (up 5.5pp). But IDC explicitly flagged Xiaomi's "challenges of transitioning its portfolio toward higher price points" as the cause of its Q4 decline. With IDC forecasting a **12.9% decline in global smartphone shipments in 2026** due to memory shortages, this segment faces near-term headwinds.

**IoT & Lifestyle Products** delivered RMB 123.2B (+18.3%) at a record **23.1% gross margin**, powered by smart home appliances (air conditioner shipments +60%), wearables (#2 globally in TWS earbuds), and tablets (+42.3% growth). The installed base of **1.07 billion connected IoT devices** — with 21.6 million users owning 5+ Xiaomi devices — creates genuine ecosystem lock-in that competitors cannot easily replicate.

**Internet Services** remains the profit engine: RMB 37.4B in revenue (+9.7%) at **76.5% gross margin**, with 754 million global MAU generating rising ARPU through advertising (RMB 28.5B, +15.2%), gaming, and fintech. Overseas internet revenue grew ~18–20% and now represents 35% of the segment. This high-margin annuity stream, built atop Xiaomi's hardware installed base, is the underappreciated gem in the business model.

## DCF valuation across three scenarios

The intrinsic value calculation uses owner earnings (approximated by FCF) discounted at rates reflecting Xiaomi's risk profile. A WACC of **9–10%** is derived from a risk-free rate of 3.5%, equity risk premium of 6.5%, beta of 0.9, and Xiaomi's low leverage, though I apply a 12% rate in the bear case to reflect elevated China-specific risks.

**Base FCF is set conservatively at RMB 25B** — between the RMB 21B (high capex) and RMB 32B (low capex) estimates — to normalize for the heavy EV investment phase.

The bear case assumes smartphone stagnation, EV margin compression from the China price war, India write-off of frozen assets, and memory cost inflation crushing margins — yielding a floor of **HKD 18.50**. The base case models EV scaling to 600K+ units by 2027 with gradual margin improvement, steady IoT growth, and internet services compounding — producing a fair value of **HKD 38.20**. The bull case envisions EV becoming China's top-5 brand by 2028, successful international EV expansion, and the premiumization flywheel lifting smartphone ASPs — reaching **HKD 62.50**.

**Graham Number**: √(22.5 × CNY 1.56 EPS × CNY 10.25 BVPS) = **CNY 18.97 → HKD 20.30**. At HKD 30.88, the stock trades 52% above the Graham Number — failing this classical deep-value screen, which is expected for a growth company.

**Earnings Power Value (EPV)**: Normalizing after-tax operating earnings at ~RMB 28B (operating income) × 0.85 (tax adjustment) = RMB 23.8B, capitalized at 10% WACC, yields EPV of RMB 238B + RMB 76B net cash = **RMB 314B (CNY 12.10/share → HKD 12.95)**. The market cap of ~RMB 748B is **2.4× EPV**, meaning investors are paying heavily for growth. This growth premium is justified only if the EV business scales as projected.

## Risks that keep this from being a high-conviction position

**India regulatory crisis** is a permanent impairment. With ₹4,820 crore ($545M) frozen and Xiaomi India effectively in strategic retreat, what was once the company's largest international market now contributes single-digit revenue share. The Karnataka High Court rejected Xiaomi's appeal, and resolution appears years away.

**Memory cost inflation** poses an immediate 2026 threat. IDC projects global smartphone shipments falling 12.9% with ASPs rising 14% due to component shortages. Xiaomi's smartphone gross margin already fell 1.7pp in FY2025, and the sub-$200 segment where Xiaomi has massive volume may become "permanently uneconomical" per IDC. Q4 2025's adjusted net profit decline of 23.7% YoY was an early warning.

**China EV price war intensification** could compress the EV segment's impressive 24.3% gross margin. The phasing in of a 5% NEV purchase tax in 2026 will squeeze demand, and BYD's dominant cost structure gives it pricing power that Xiaomi cannot match. Xiaomi's CFO stated volumes take priority over margins — suggesting willingness to sacrifice profitability for scale.

**Geopolitical risk** remains moderate. While Xiaomi was successfully removed from the US military blacklist in 2021, re-escalation under shifting US-China dynamics is possible. More critically, Xiaomi's EVs have zero international revenue — European entry is being explored for 2027, but tariff barriers and political resistance to Chinese EVs make expansion uncertain.

**Share dilution** is a recurring concern. The March 2025 $5.3B placement diluted shareholders by ~3.2%, and stock-based compensation continues. While FY2025 buybacks of HKD 6.3B partially offset this, net dilution persists.

## Conclusion: BUY with discipline at the 52-week floor

Xiaomi at HKD 30.88 is not a classic Graham deep-value play — it fails the Graham Number test and trades at 2.4× earnings power value. But for a growth-aware value investor willing to adapt the framework, the setup is attractive: a **trailing P/E of 17.4× on 44% adjusted profit growth**, a PEG ratio of **0.70**, net cash of RMB 76B covering 10% of the market cap, and an EV business that went from zero to RMB 103B revenue in 20 months while turning profitable.

The probability-weighted fair value of **~HKD 39** offers a **21.5% margin of safety** — below the textbook 33% threshold but notable given that 84% of analysts rate the stock a Buy with a consensus target of HKD 44.85. The stock is sitting at its 52-week low following a 50% drawdown, with peak pessimism around Q4 margin pressure and 2026 smartphone headwinds largely priced in.
