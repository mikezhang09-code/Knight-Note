# Fosun International FY2025: deep value or deep trouble?

**Fosun International reported a headline loss of RMB 23.4 billion for FY2025, but this number masks a business whose core operations are quietly strengthening.** The loss — driven entirely by non-cash impairments on real estate and legacy goodwill — represents what Chairman Guo Guangchang calls "repairing the roof on a sunny day." Beneath the accounting clean-up, Fosun's four core subsidiaries grew their revenue share to 74% of the group total, Fosun Pharma's net profit rose 22%, and the insurance businesses delivered their best-ever results. At **HK$4.14 per share** against a stated adjusted NAV of **HKD 18.1**, the stock trades at a **77% discount** — among the deepest conglomerate discounts globally. For a Graham-Buffett investor, the question is whether this margin of safety is real or illusory.

---

## FY2025 financial statements show a tale of two companies

Fosun's FY2025 results, announced March 30, 2026, reveal a stark divergence between headline accounting and underlying operations.

**Total revenue fell 9.7% to RMB 173.43 billion** (from RMB 192.14 billion in FY2024), largely because Yuyuan's gold jewelry business contracted amid volatile gold prices and Fosun continued divesting non-core assets. Adjusted industrial operating profit — management's preferred measure of underlying performance — came in at **RMB 4.0 billion**, down 18.4% from RMB 4.9 billion, reflecting the revenue decline but still firmly positive.

The headline **RMB 23.4 billion net loss** was almost entirely driven by one-off non-cash impairments: approximately **55% related to real estate project write-downs** (~RMB 12.9 billion) and **45% related to goodwill and intangible assets** of non-core segments (~RMB 10.5 billion). Management emphasized these provisions do not affect cash flow or operations. A profit warning on March 6 had guided for a loss of RMB 21.5–23.5 billion, and the stock actually rallied over 10% between the warning and results day, suggesting the market had already digested the write-down.

The decline in cash from RMB 106 billion to RMB 61 billion warrants attention, though the company maintains **RMB 144.6 billion in unutilized banking facilities** and confirmed positive operating cash flow for the year. The debt-to-capital ratio worsened to 57% from 52%, partly reflecting the equity erosion from impairments rather than new borrowing.

## Per-share metrics and the dividend surprise

With approximately **8.17 billion shares outstanding**, the RMB 23.4 billion loss translates to an estimated basic loss per share of roughly **RMB 2.86** (approximately HKD 3.14). This makes trailing P/E meaningless for valuation purposes.

Fosun proposed a **special dividend of HK$0.20 per share** for FY2025, marking the company's 30th listing anniversary — a tenfold increase from the token HKD 0.02 paid for FY2024. More importantly, management committed to raising the dividend payout ratio from 20% to **35% starting FY2026**, with total dividends of at least **HKD 1.5 billion** for the 2026 financial year. The company also announced a share buyback program and pledged that controlling shareholders and senior management would increase their holdings by up to **HKD 500 million** within 12 months.

The book value per share on an IFRS basis (total equity attributable to shareholders) is estimated at approximately **HKD 23** after the impairments reduced equity from ~RMB 197 billion at mid-2025. The company's adjusted NAV per share of **HKD 18.1** uses a more conservative measure that applies market valuations to listed holdings and recent transaction prices to unlisted assets, minus group-level net debt.

## Segment performance: pharma and insurance carry the conglomerate

Fosun's four core subsidiaries generated **RMB 128.2 billion in revenue**, representing 74% of the group total (up from 70% in FY2024). The concentration into higher-quality businesses is the centerpiece of Fosun's "streamlining and strengthening" strategy.

**Health (Fosun Pharma)** delivered the strongest results. Revenue grew modestly to **RMB 41.5 billion** (+1.4%), but net profit surged **21.7% to RMB 3.371 billion** as the product mix shifted toward higher-margin innovative drugs. Innovative drug revenue hit **RMB 9.9 billion** (+29.6%), and overseas revenue reached RMB 13.0 billion (+14.9%). Gross margins expanded 2.1 percentage points to 49.9%. Subsidiary Henlius (2696.HK) posted revenue of RMB 6.7 billion and net profit of RMB 827 million in its third consecutive profitable year. On the business development front, Fosun Pharma signed licensing deals with Pfizer (>$2 billion potential), Clavis Bio (up to $7.25 billion), and Eisai (>$300 million), positioning the pharma arm as a genuine global platform.

**Happiness (Tourism & Lifestyle)** presented a mixed picture. Club Med achieved **record performance** with revenue of **RMB 18.07 billion** (+3.6%) and operating profit of **RMB 1.44 billion** (+4.6%) across 67 resorts worldwide. However, Yuyuan was a significant drag, facing an expected full-year loss of approximately **RMB 4.8 billion** due to structural shifts in China's gold jewelry consumption market. Fosun Tourism Group was privatized and delisted from HKEX in March 2025, giving Fosun 100% ownership of Club Med and Atlantis Sanya.

**Wealth (Insurance & Asset Management)** was arguably the most improved segment. Fosun Insurance Portugal (Fidelidade) earned a net profit of **EUR 201 million** (+15.8%) and received an inaugural **"A" rating from S&P**. Pramerica Fosun Life Insurance delivered explosive growth with premium income of **RMB 13.28 billion** (+41.6%) and net profit of **RMB 650 million** — a nearly sixfold increase. Fosun United Health Insurance grew insurance income 50% to RMB 7.84 billion with net profit of RMB 139 million, marking five consecutive profitable years.

**Intelligent Manufacturing (Resources & Industrial)** saw Hainan Mining operationalize the Bougouni lithium mine in Mali, producing 45,000 tons of lithium concentrate. The first shipment of 30,000 tons arrived at China's Yangpu Port in January 2026. Hainan Mining is also expanding into oil and gas in Oman, building a "minerals + energy" network spanning West Africa, the Middle East, and Southeast Asia.

## NAV analysis reveals a 77% discount to stated asset values

The sum-of-parts calculation is the core of any value argument for Fosun. Management states an **adjusted NAV of RMB 133.5 billion (HKD 18.1 per share)**, while the market capitalizes the company at just **HKD 33.8 billion**. This implies a **77% discount to NAV**.

An independent reconstruction of the listed portfolio broadly supports management's figures:

| Listed Subsidiary | Market Cap | Fosun's Stake | Attributed Value (HKD) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fosun Pharma (600196.SS / 2196.HK) | ~CNY 65B | ~36% | ~HKD 25.7B |
| Yuyuan Inc (600655.SS) | ~CNY 22.4B | ~62% | ~HKD 15.3B |
| Hainan Mining (601969.SS) | ~CNY 22.1B | ~60% | ~HKD 14.6B |
| **Listed subtotal** | | | **~HKD 55.6B** |

Adding estimated values for unlisted and privatized assets substantially increases the total. Fosun Insurance Portugal (Fidelidade), with EUR 201 million in annual profit and a 30% Portuguese market share, could reasonably be valued at **HKD 30–40 billion** for Fosun's ~85% stake using comparable insurance multiples. The fully-owned Fosun Tourism Group (Club Med + Atlantis Sanya) contributes an estimated **HKD 10–15 billion** in equity value. Other holdings — Lanvin Group (~67% stake), Wolverhampton Wanderers FC, and various private investments — add perhaps **HKD 15–25 billion**. The total gross asset value ranges from approximately **HKD 110–136 billion** before deducting group-level net debt, directionally consistent with the stated HKD 148 billion adjusted NAV.

The stock's **P/B ratio of 0.24x** and **price-to-sales of 0.15x** are extreme by any standard. For context, Citi maintained a Buy rating with a **HKD 6.50 target** (57% upside), while Guotai Haitong initiated coverage at **Buy with HKD 7.24** and an NAV estimate matching management's HKD 18.1. The analyst consensus target sits at approximately **HKD 5.49**, implying 33% upside from the current price.

## Debt reduction is progressing but remains the central risk

Fosun has divested approximately **RMB 75 billion in assets** over recent years as part of its deleveraging campaign. FY2024 alone saw RMB 17.5 billion in signed group-level asset divestments and RMB 30 billion at the consolidated level. A notable 2025 transaction was the sale of a 40% stake in Portuguese hospital operator Luz Saúde for **EUR 310 million**.

The refinancing pipeline has been active. In September 2025, Fosun issued a **USD 400 million bond** (4-year tenor). In November 2025, it returned to the Euro bond market after a four-year absence with a **EUR 400 million bond** (5-year, 5.875% coupon). In March 2026 — just days before the results — Fosun secured a **$500 million refinancing loan** despite the loss warning, signaling continued bank confidence. The company faces a near-term maturity of its **USD 5% May 2026 bond** (XS2343337122), which will be a key test.

Management's medium-term targets are ambitious: reduce group-level debt to **below RMB 60 billion**, recover **RMB 60 billion in cash** at group level, restore annual profit to **~RMB 10 billion**, and achieve an **investment-grade credit rating**. S&P currently rates Fosun at **BB- with Stable outlook**, though it noted in March 2026 that the asset write-down "weakens credit quality." The gap between BB- and investment grade (BBB-) is meaningful and likely requires several years of execution.

The deterioration of the debt-to-capital ratio from 52% to 57% in a single year, combined with the sharp decline in cash from RMB 106 billion to RMB 61 billion, underscores that deleveraging is not yet on a firmly downward trajectory at the consolidated level. However, group-level debt (holdco obligations) is the more relevant figure for credit analysis, and the RMB 60 billion reduction target specifically addresses this.

## Eight risks a Graham-Buffett investor must weigh

**Structural conglomerate discount.** Chinese holding companies historically trade at 30–50% discounts to NAV. Fosun's 77% discount is extreme even by those standards, but Stockopedia classifies it as a potential **"value trap"** — low multiples that persist without catalysts for rerating. The complex corporate structure with dozens of subsidiaries, cross-holdings, and opaque unlisted assets makes it genuinely difficult for outside investors to assess true economic value.

**Refinancing dependency.** With sub-investment-grade credit (BB-) and bond coupons ranging from 5.875% to 8.5%, Fosun pays significantly above investment-grade borrowing costs. The business model relies on continuous access to capital markets and bank syndication. Any disruption — credit market freeze, sovereign risk repricing, or loss of bank relationships — could create acute stress.

**China real estate overhang.** Though Fosun has now written down RMB 12.9 billion in real estate assets, residual exposure persists through Yuyuan and related entities. China's property market shows no sign of structural recovery, and further impairments cannot be ruled out.

**Key-man risk.** Fosun remains inextricable from founder Guo Guangchang. Any personal, regulatory, or health event affecting Guo could materially impact the group's strategic direction and market confidence.

**Holdco cash flow mismatch.** S&P and Moody's have historically flagged that dividends upstreamed from subsidiaries may be insufficient to cover the holdco's interest and operating expenses. Much of the RMB 61 billion in group cash sits within regulated subsidiaries (insurance, pharma) and cannot be freely moved to the parent.

Additional risks include ongoing China macro headwinds affecting ~45% of revenue, regulatory unpredictability for Chinese private conglomerates, and currency exposure from the 55% overseas revenue base.

## Q1 2026 catalysts suggest management is pushing for a rerating

Several developments in early 2026 signal that management is actively working to narrow the discount. Bloomberg reported on March 25 that **Club Med is considering a Hong Kong IPO**, which would crystallize value in Fosun's most prized wholly-owned asset and provide a market-determined valuation for a business that was privatized just one year ago. This is arguably the most significant potential catalyst.

The **MSCI ESG rating upgrade to AAA** (the highest possible score) on March 26 may expand the investor universe eligible to hold the stock. Fosun also received recognition from the HKMA for ESG reporting excellence and was included in the FTSE4Good Index for the fifth consecutive year.

On the pharma front, Fosun Pharma's licensing pipeline — with potential deal values exceeding **$9.5 billion combined** across the Pfizer, Clavis Bio, and Eisai agreements — represents substantial option value not captured in current earnings. If even a fraction of these milestone payments materializes, the health segment's contribution could step-change.

The shareholder return initiatives — special dividend, buyback program, and insider buying commitment — collectively amount to potential capital return exceeding **HKD 3 billion** (buyback + shareholder purchases + special dividend), representing roughly 9% of the current market cap. This is a level of capital return commitment unusual for a Chinese conglomerate trading at distressed multiples.

## Conclusion

Fosun International presents a textbook Graham-Buffett tension between quantitative cheapness and qualitative complexity. The stock trades at **0.24x book value** with an adjusted NAV of **HKD 18.1 per share** against a price of **HKD 4.14** — a 77% discount that implies the market assigns near-zero value to unlisted assets and deeply discounts even the listed portfolio. Core operating businesses generated **RMB 4 billion in adjusted profit** and are demonstrably improving: Fosun Pharma is becoming a genuine global pharmaceutical platform, the insurance businesses are hitting their stride, and Club Med is at record profitability.

The critical question for a value investor is not whether the assets are cheap — they manifestly are — but whether the catalysts exist to close the discount and whether the balance sheet can withstand the wait. The **57% debt-to-capital ratio**, **BB- credit rating**, and **RMB 45 billion cash decline** in a single year are real concerns. Yet the $500 million refinancing secured in March, the return to Euro bond markets, and the unused RMB 144.6 billion in credit facilities suggest the liquidity bridge remains intact.

The most distinctive feature of the current setup is the alignment of multiple potential catalysts: a Club Med IPO that could reprice the tourism portfolio, a pharmaceutical licensing pipeline worth billions in milestones, a credible dividend increase trajectory, and management buying alongside shareholders. If Fosun delivers on even its medium-term profit target of RMB 10 billion and reduces holdco debt below RMB 60 billion, a rerating toward even a 50% NAV discount would imply a stock price above **HKD 9** — more than double current levels. The margin of safety is substantial if the thesis holds, but the conglomerate structure and Chinese macro backdrop demand position sizing discipline commensurate with the risks.
