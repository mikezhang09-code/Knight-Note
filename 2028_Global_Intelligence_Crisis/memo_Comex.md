James van Geelen’s “2028 Global Intelligence Crisis” memo is a thought‑experiment about how rapid AI adoption could trigger a white‑collar depression, a deflationary shock, and a financial crisis, even though he says it is a scenario, not his base‑case forecast. [bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-24/citrini-founder-shocked-his-ai-prediction-spurred-stocks-selloff)

## Main ideas in the memo

- **AI as a substitute, not just a tool**  
  The memo posits that by around 2028, “machine intelligence” becomes a broadly competent, cheap substitute for human cognitive labor across a wide range of white‑collar tasks, not merely an assistive tool. This erodes what he calls the historical “intelligence premium” humans have earned in the economy. [the-independent](https://www.the-independent.com/news/world/americas/ai-doomsday-report-citrini-b2926763.html)

- **“Ghost GDP” and the death of friction**  
  Van Geelen introduces “ghost GDP” to describe economic output produced by AI systems that does not show up as traditional wages or jobs, because the work is done by software rather than employees. He argues that many service sectors—tax prep, insurance renewal, financial advice, and any “I navigate complexity you find tedious” business—are built on **friction**, and AI agents will drive that friction, and therefore those business moats, toward zero. [fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/23/will-ai-take-my-job-cause-recession-crash-james-val-geelen-citrini/)

- **White‑collar recession and deflationary cascade**  
  In the memo’s scenario, companies respond to AI by aggressively cutting white‑collar headcount, which pushes unemployment above 10%, compresses consumption, and leads to a “deflationary cascade” where prices and asset values fall together. The shock is concentrated in educated, prime‑age workers with high credit scores, unlike prior recessions that hit lower‑income labor first. [economictimes](https://economictimes.com/markets/us-stocks/news/decoded-the-viral-doomsday-ai-memo-that-roiled-wall-street/articleshow/128773322.cms)

- **Financial system stress: mortgages and private credit**  
  He highlights second‑order effects on credit markets: the residential mortgage system struggles as high‑FICO borrowers see permanent income impairment, while private credit and PE‑backed software firms face defaults because their customers use AI to replace expensive SaaS and services. This creates a channel from labor disruption into systemic financial risk. [finance.yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/software-payments-shares-tumble-citrini-162303649.html)

- **Market concentration and named corporate losers**  
  The memo calls out specific sectors and tickers—software, payments, platforms like ServiceNow, DoorDash, American Express and others—as vulnerable to AI agents that bypass intermediaries and fees. Because current markets are heavily concentrated in AI‑beneficiary tech stocks, this narrative can easily trigger a sharp de‑risking if investors suddenly price in displacement risks. [economictimes](https://economictimes.com/markets/us-stocks/news/citrini-founder-shocked-his-ai-prediction-spurred-stocks-selloff/articleshow/128766490.cms)

- **Scenario, not base case**  
  Van Geelen stresses that this is a “thought exercise” designed to surface a tail risk, not his official forecast, and that his base case remains that AI will mostly augment rather than fully replace workers. He says they could not assign a 0% probability to such an outcome, which is why they chose to publish. [fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/23/will-ai-take-my-job-cause-recession-crash-james-val-geelen-citrini/)

## Conceptual framing and philosophy

- **Human intelligence as the scarce input**  
  He frames economic history as built around scarcity of human intelligence; capital and energy became abundant relative to the bottleneck of qualified minds. If AI removes that bottleneck, the “ghost in the machine” upends existing models of productivity, labor bargaining power, and returns to education. [finance.yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ghost-gdp-white-collar-recession-163043617.html)

- **Second‑order thinking and “sword of Damocles”**  
  Van Geelen draws on his reputation for second‑order thinking—looking beyond immediate boosts to earnings toward structural labor and credit effects. He has described an AI‑driven white‑collar recession as a “sword of Damocles” hanging over knowledge workers, which may fall quickly once AI crosses certain capability thresholds. [marketwatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/this-top-stock-picker-spotted-nvidia-and-glp-1s-early-and-made-over-200-heres-what-hes-buying-now-16a5c5fa)

- **Fictional memo as stress test**  
  Structurally, the essay is written as a fictional June 2028 research memo looking back over the crisis, a narrative device meant to force readers—especially investors and policymakers—to take the scenario seriously as a risk case and to think through transmission channels. [sources](https://sources.news/p/the-ai-doomer-essay-that-rattled)

## Immediate market and policy implications

- **Market reaction and repricing risk**  
  The memo went viral, contributing to a sharp selloff in software, payments and AI‑exposed names, including IBM’s worst drop in decades, as investors briefly rotated out of perceived AI beneficiaries and fee‑taking intermediaries. The speed of this reaction shows how sensitive current valuations are to narratives about AI’s downside, not just its growth potential. [ndtvprofit](https://www.ndtvprofit.com/technology/citrini-founder-shocked-his-ai-prediction-spurred-stocks-selloff-11132686)

- **Re‑examining AI‑exposed business models**  
  For corporates, the memo pushes boards and management to ask whether their margins depend on informational asymmetry, complexity, or transaction friction that AI agents can cheaply eliminate. Sectors like professional services, financial advisors, insurance brokers, certain software vendors, and gig‑economy intermediaries are implicitly being told to redesign value propositions around irreplaceable human elements or unique data, not paperwork and complexity. [economictimes](https://economictimes.com/markets/us-stocks/news/decoded-the-viral-doomsday-ai-memo-that-roiled-wall-street/articleshow/128773322.cms)

- **Labor, education, and social safety nets**  
  For policymakers, the scenario suggests that traditional tools—focused on cushioning blue‑collar shocks—may be misaligned if a future crisis primarily hits white‑collar professionals and highly educated workers. It raises questions about retraining, income insurance, housing finance design, and whether existing unemployment systems are suited to large, fast white‑collar displacements. [finance.yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ghost-gdp-white-collar-recession-163043617.html)

- **Macro and credit risk management**  
  For regulators and central banks, his narrative functions like an informal stress‑test pointing at concentration risk in AI‑themed equities, vulnerability in mortgage portfolios concentrated in high‑income borrowers, and fragilities in private credit tied to software and services. Even if the specific path is unlikely, it argues that risk models should explicitly include rapid AI‑driven employment shocks as a non‑zero‑probability tail event. [finance.yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/software-payments-shares-tumble-citrini-162303649.html)

## Strategic takeaways for investors and firms

- **Do not ignore low‑probability, high‑impact AI paths**  
  The central implication is that investors and executives should treat AI not only as a growth driver but as a source of structural demand destruction and credit risk in certain scenarios. Portfolio construction and corporate strategy need to account for both the upside (productivity, margins) and downside (labor displacement, fee compression, defaults). [fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/23/will-ai-take-my-job-cause-recession-crash-james-val-geelen-citrini/)

- **Favor businesses with durable, non‑friction moats**  
  The memo implicitly favors companies whose edge is tied to scarce real assets, unique data, regulation, or physical networks instead of pure information friction and transactional complexity. For example, firms that integrate AI to enhance products tied to physical goods, regulated infrastructure, or deep proprietary datasets may be better positioned than those who simply sit between customers and commodity services. [economictimes](https://economictimes.com/markets/us-stocks/news/decoded-the-viral-doomsday-ai-memo-that-roiled-wall-street/articleshow/128773322.cms)

- **Use the scenario as a planning tool, not a prediction**  
  Van Geelen’s own clarification—that he is still invested in AI winners and is “not allocated for a financial crisis”—underscores that he is not calling for immediate collapse. The report’s practical value is as a framework to test assumptions about labor, credit, and business model resilience in a world where intelligence is abundant and cheap.
