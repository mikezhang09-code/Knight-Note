## The six moats, decoded

**Microsoft** has arguably the widest and deepest moat of the group. Its operating margin sits at 45.6% — the highest among the six — reflecting extraordinary pricing power and switching costs. The key: enterprise lock-in. Once an organization adopts Office 365, Azure, and Windows together, the cost of migrating is enormous. Azure surpassed $75 billion in annual revenue for the first time, growing 34%, and operating income reached $128.5 billion, up 17% for FY2025. Microsoft's AI moat via Copilot (embedded across its entire stack) and the OpenAI/Anthropic partnerships gives it a unique platform position — it monetizes AI on *someone else's model breakthroughs*.

**Apple's** moat is the hardware-software-services flywheel. FY2025 revenue hit $416 billion with a new September quarter record of $102.5 billion, while the installed base of active devices reached an all-time high across every product category. The Services segment (~$100B+ annualized) earns 74% gross margins — essentially a recurring toll road on 1.8 billion devices. The weakness: heavy dependence on iPhone in a maturing smartphone market, and relatively late to the AI race.

**Alphabet** combines search monopoly, YouTube dominance, and an accelerating cloud business. Full-year 2025 revenue exceeded $400 billion for the first time, with Google Cloud revenue growing 48% to $17.7 billion in Q4 alone. Its moat is data-driven: every search query trains better AI, which attracts more users, which generates more ad revenue. The Gemini AI family now has 750 million monthly active users. The concern is regulatory: ongoing antitrust proceedings could force structural changes to its search distribution.

**Meta** has transformed itself into an AI-powered advertising juggernaut. Full-year 2025 revenue reached $201 billion, growing 22% year-over-year, with ad impressions up 12% and average price per ad up 9%. Its social graph of 3.6 billion daily users is almost unreplicable. AI-driven Reels recommendations boosted Instagram watch time by 30%. The trade-off: massive capex ($72 billion in 2025, guided to $115-135 billion in 2026) on AI infrastructure and Reality Labs losses create execution risk.

**Tencent** operates China's "everything platform." WeChat/Weixin reached 1.414 billion monthly active users, and Q3 2025 revenues hit RMB 192.9 billion ($27.1 billion), up 15% year-over-year. Its moat is the super-app ecosystem: messaging, payments, gaming, cloud, advertising — all interconnected. Non-IFRS operating margin expanded to 38% with operating profit growing 18%. Tencent also sits atop a massive investment portfolio (listed investee companies worth ~$110B), creating optionality beyond its operations. The risk is geopolitical and regulatory.

**Alibaba** has the weakest moat of the six. While still China's e-commerce leader, fiscal year 2025 non-GAAP net income of RMB 158 billion essentially flatlined year-over-year, and free cash flow dropped 53% to RMB 73.9 billion as it invested aggressively in quick commerce and AI infrastructure. It faces intense competition from Pinduoduo, Douyin, and JD.com. The bright spot is Alibaba Cloud, which holds the largest market share in Asia-Pacific and saw triple-digit AI-related product revenue growth for seven consecutive quarters.

## Investment verdict

I'm not a financial advisor, but here's how the data stacks up for consideration:

**Alphabet (GOOGL) appears to offer the most compelling risk/reward profile today.** It trades at a P/E of ~28.5 — a moderate premium to its 5-year average of ~24.5 — but delivers 15% revenue growth with a 32% operating margin that's expanding. Its cloud business is inflecting (48% Q4 growth), YouTube passed $60 billion in revenue, and the Gemini ecosystem is gaining massive traction. It has the widest diversification of AI monetization paths (Search AI, Cloud AI, YouTube AI, Waymo) of any company in this group. The antitrust overhang actually provides a *discount* to what would otherwise be a much higher multiple.

**Tencent (0700.HK) is the strongest value play among the six.** At a P/E of ~22 — well below its 10-year median of ~28 — it delivers 15% growth with 33-38% margins, an unassailable super-app ecosystem, and a hidden asset portfolio worth over $150 billion (listed + unlisted). The China discount is real but arguably overdone for a company with these fundamentals and shareholder returns (aggressive buybacks).

**Meta** offers the highest growth (22%) but demands faith in massive capex payoff. **Microsoft** is the steadiest compounder but priced for perfection at ~25x earnings. **Apple** at 32x P/E is the most expensive relative to its single-digit growth — you're paying for the moat, not the growth. **Alibaba** is cheap (~23x) for a reason: its moat is eroding and margins are under pressure from competitive investment.

The core takeaway: **excess profits come from moats, but investability depends on whether the market has already priced them in.** Alphabet and Tencent currently offer the best gap between moat quality and market pricing.
